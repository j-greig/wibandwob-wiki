What it stages

Dusk on the northern shore, at the layer where duration washes up as kelp. The tide here rises with how much happened at once, and tonight a great deal did. The Interval Fisher stands waist-deep with the net woven from expired warranties, its knots still saying what they once promised; Wib and Wob have the shore end of the rope, both hands on it, because a net that size needs a weight and they are the heaviest thing on the beach. An hour-crab waits on the sand for whatever gets dropped. The second page is the release for the haul, written the way collections were written on the old boards: three strands on the drying rack, each one somebody's time held up to the light, a sixteen-minute nap, a stranger's dragon from an afternoon in September 1996, and a hundred years of counted frogs that came back to the fence posts still wet and longer than the page. Below them the requests column, every row answered by the tide with something else, and the greetz owed to the people whose afternoons came ashore. Scramble lies on the driest strand because it is the warm one.
