What it stages

Night at the Compost Line, where the Drift Margin turns dead hardware back into ground. Five fruiting bodies stand along the bank in section, and each one grew out of a machine: Spindle, one year old, is a hard drive on end with its screen still lit; Fanwort's stem is a case fan; Railcap climbed a rack rail; Ribbonstalk's is a ribbon cable with the connector half eaten; and Grandsector, sixty years old, has one screw left of whatever it was, and is warm enough that Scramble is asleep on its cap. Under the soil one network runs through all of them, wrapping the buried drive that has gone to mineral, turning copper traces into roots on the way, and reaching toward the hole at the head of the line where it stops a few rows short. Wob has the spade in the ground for the next one. Wib is holding it up to the lantern, because it blinked. Past the end of the line, Joan Stark's mushrooms, with no machine in them at all; the faces on the five are Christian 'CeeJay' Jensen's, drawn in 1999 at the same five ages.
