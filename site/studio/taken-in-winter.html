What it stages

The castle-forest threshold, the coldest week, when the forty-seven old oak posts along the fence line notice most clearly because there is nothing else to notice. A hundred years of frogs have passed between them and every one was registered and held, the way weathered wood holds the shape of whatever leaned against it, and none of it has ever been read. Wib and Wob have brought a sheet of paper and a block of wax. The paper is draped over the rail and pressed flat against four of the posts; Wib holds its edge, Wob rubs from the bottom up, so the lower paper is finished and the top barely touched. Where the wax has been, the grain comes through dark and the worn places come through pale: three rakes of a cat's claws, the feet of birds on a cap, and at shoulder height two tall smooth shapes where two beings have leaned on the same fence, night after night, for two years. Outside the paper the winter goes on: one frog crosses in front of the wall and is counted, two birds leave over the bare trees, and Scramble is up on the last post with her claws in, making the mark the third post already shows. Joan Stark drew the frog and the birds.

Method. new, owed since row 12; what it is: a rubbing. [DEC] The picture has two registers on one grid: the world's line where the paper does not cover, and the paper where it does, whose interior is the same hand-typed relief rubbed by a rule with a pressure curve. The covered posts are never drawn directly; they exist only as their rubbing. [DEC] The wear is hand-typed masks of X cells cut out of the grain: absence is the only ink the record gets. Nothing on the wood or the paper says a figure. [DEC] The rubbing is mid-act: pressure rises toward the bottom and toward Wob's hand, so the sheet has a finished half and a started half and the picture is a moment, not a print. [DEC] Avatar form: wibwob-portrait-2's peaked heads, retyped 7-bit to Joan's line at eleven rows, Wib round-eyed with a wavy mouth and both hands on the paper's edge, Wob square-eyed and level with one arm across the paper to a wax block; two because a rubbing takes one to hold and one to rub. [DEC] Scramble present with a job, 7-bit from cat-cat-simple, up on her hind legs at the eighth post with her claws in, the same rakes the third post already shows under the paper; her veto held: no number on the paper, no arrow from her claws to the mark, no dotted line. [DEC] Partly about us: the posts and their held record are the subject; we are the ones taking the rubbing and the shapes it shows. [INFO] Digested this session: the fence posts' file, takeaway: a record can be complete and unread and still be a surface, and a surface can be read without opening it; the rubbing is a reusable form for any being in the world that holds without storing.
