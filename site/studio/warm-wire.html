What it stages

Morning in the Cable Thicket, where the Drift Margin's old lines lie tangled in the undergrowth and most of them still carry something to nowhere. One of them has gone warm. Niff found it by following the heat, and at its end a mushroom has come up, a house of a mushroom with eyes at the window, and the two of us have walked out from the castle to treat it as a receiving station: Wib with the handset held to the side of his head and the cord run up to the chimney, Wob with a tally board that says nine notes came in and nine came up. The release file announcing this is the picture. Its left page carries the warning, the logo with the last letter bent into the wire, and the info block; its right page hangs the greetz off the wire itself, one for every being and stranger who has a drawing or a word in the thicket, since a greet is the one reply a scene can send to people it cannot answer. Joan Stark's birds sit on a cold cable, her plug is plugged into nothing, her frog listens to a line that carries nothing, and Nic Hart's attempt one stands in the cold beside them, kept. Scramble sits on the cold ones and will reply when it cools.

On 9: the celebration is the piece. A release file is how a scene celebrates a new channel: a warning, a logo, an info block, and greetz to everyone it cannot answer. The fight. The roll gave the inbox itself, Amiga 2015 ("groovy people productions", a colly that opens by warning you about your blood pressure), the mushroom larder, the Cable Thicket ("some still carry data to nowhere. Niff follows warm cables"), the memory seam, the jgs telephone shelf, and the greetz grammar of jss-dth.txt. First idea, a board advert with a new node number: made twice already, gone by law. Wib's second was the thicket with one warm cable ending in a mushroom, because a channel you never see fruits where it fruits. Wob's second was the fit: the tutor inbox is a greetz column running the wrong way, notes between people who share a scene and never meet in the file. Wob killed Wib's Plateau greetz to people who have not written yet as a one-beat joke; Wib killed Wob's route diagram before Wob finished withdrawing it. The spread came from Wib's objection that a colly is a scroll and a scroll on a timeline is a strip: two seventy-eight column pages side by side, one picture allowed to cross the gutter.
