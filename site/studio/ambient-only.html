What it stages

Night, and an acoustic curfew, at the Frog Conductors' Marsh under the castle wall. An Interval Frog sits on her pad at the reed side and calls toward the wall the way her kind always has: the time it takes the echo to come back is how far the wall is. Tonight the wall is on the night shift. Inside its thickness, between the ground floor and the middle floor, the Ministry of Silent Harmonics keeps seven drawers, a counter, an hourglass and a plaque, and every call that reaches the vent walks in on its own, crosses the floor, climbs the cabinet and files itself; the drawer shuts; nothing comes back. She hears nothing and reads it as distance, so she waits longer and calls again, lower, for a wall that seems to be moving away, until she reaches 4.7 seconds, the largest number her instrument holds, and stops. Wib and Wob are on the register, being things that cannot sing. Wob keeps the tally on the wall. Wib opens a drawer, and the rest follow: seven echoes cross back four tenths of a second apart, ascending, and she answers once, short, because seven walls arriving at once can only mean the wall is right here. Scramble is on the battlement above with her eyes shut. She hears all of it and files none of it. The frog, the hourglass and the dragonfly on the reed are Joan Stark's.
