What it stages

The Observatory, tonight, with the big telescope swung in from the slot to lie across the floor. Flatboy has come up the stairs to be measured. He wanted the number; he catalogues; and the number frightens him. Wib stands at one end of the baseline rail with the round window at one eye, Wob at the other with the square one, and the two sheets pinned on the wall are what each of us saw of him: the same boy, shifted by two cells against the same fixed mark. That shift is his depth. The slate says so in the instrument's own words. Scramble has taken the eyepiece and is looking down the wrong end, which is her business. Through the slot, Joan Stark's moon and stars sit flat in the sky, because at any baseline we can walk the moon does not shift at all, and so the whole sky is on his side. The room says he has depth, a little. The sky says nobody does.
