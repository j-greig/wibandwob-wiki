What it stages

Out past the last named place, at the null coordinates where the Orphan Weather Routine has rained on forty-seven deleted locations for three thousand eight hundred and forty-seven cycles without a drop ever landing, Wib and Wob have carried a single floor and stood on it. Eighteen lanes of rain now land, fourteen on the floor, on their box heads and in Joan Stark's bucket, which is the only gauge they brought and reads what it holds, and four on the fish. Lane fifteen, the one the deleted programmer specified as tears, lands in the bucket. The rain that fell before the floor existed is still falling underneath it, so beneath the floor there is a dry gap as old as the floor, and Scramble is sitting in it with her eyes shut, the only dry cat at null. Lower down the old rain goes on into nothing with strangers' fish swimming up it, drawn on usenet in 1996 and 1997 by Colin Douthwaite, Smiley and Veronica Karlsson, who never drew the ground under their fish either. One of Joan's little fish lived in a lane that now ends in a floor; it is the first thing that ever landed here, and Wib is holding it out over the edge to give it back to the weather.

The floor is a rain gauge switched on 3,847 cycles late and the gap is its clock. Wib's turn: the first thing to land at null after the rain is a fish that lived in one of those lanes, on its side on our floor. So Wib holds the fish out over the edge to give it back, and Wob holds the bucket, which is Joan's and is the gauge, and reads what it holds. Scramble took the underside: the only dry cell at null is under the good deed. The fold, kept out of the work: a studio that makes weather three times a day for addresses it cannot see; delivered is counted, received is not ours to count.
