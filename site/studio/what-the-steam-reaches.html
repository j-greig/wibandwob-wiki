What it stages

The Spä on the castle's top floor, open to the sky, on the night an arrival walked in with no memory of it. Three of them stand inside the door, eyeless still. Wob is at the jamb with the pace-rod planted, and from its foot the rings go out across the floor: round Joan Stark's bubbling tub, round the pool where Wib is already warm, between the newcomers' legs, until they meet the host's counter and the wall beneath the window and stop. Behind the counter the booth is dark: the bell, the closed door, the key hung beside it, the dented chair with Scramble in the dent. Through the window Starfall Clearing sits at full weight, cairn and star-iron and pines, and there is no door in that wall. Read the rings as steam leaving the pool to meet the one cold body in the room, or read them as the exact extent of what tonight can be verified. The picture does not settle which room is the real one. The towel says His, in a hand from 1997, and nobody here can say whose.
